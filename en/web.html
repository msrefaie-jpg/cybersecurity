Memo Explanations

A simplified explanation of everything in the Internet Search section of the memo (29 questions).

01. Google Search Tricks — Search Like a Pro

The words you type in the search field are called Search Terms, and the search engine does not scan the whole network in real time — it searches its pre-stored Indexes (beware the "comprehensively scans the network" trap!).

📚 Analogy: A search engine is a librarian who has prepared a huge catalog of every book in advance — when you ask, they don't run between the shelves; they open their ready-made catalog. That's why results appear in fractions of a second.
🎯

Narrowing and Expanding Results

Advanced search is used to Narrow your results according to specific criteria ✅ p.37 · The OR operator expands the search ✅ p.28 · A hyphen (-) before a word reduces results by excluding it ✅ p.26 (the material's example: Mustang -cars).

🔗

The link: Syntax

To know who cited a Washington Post article on their site: Link: washingtonpost.com ✅ principle at p.27 — the material's own example: link: nytimes.com "to find all the sites that link to it".

👯

The related: Syntax

To find a website similar to eBay: related: eBay.com ✅ principle at p.28 — the material's example: related:amazon.com (results: Barnes & Noble and Target).

🔒

Secure Shopping

Sensitive activities (banking and shopping) require HTTPS ✅ p.20 — it uses SSL/TLS encryption on port 443 instead of HTTP's port 80. And the technical "gateway" to a web server's resources is the Port ✅ p.22 (literally!).

🌐

Saving for the Web

A document is saved for viewing on the web with the html extension — "HMT" in the question is a spelling trap! ⚠️ The memo's answer; not stated verbatim in the material (the web server stores HTML documents — p.19).

☁️

OneDrive in the Browser

OneDrive can be used as a cloud drive from the App Launcher icon ⚠️ the memo's answer — the material shows OneDrive via office.com (p.49–50) without the words "App Launcher".

02. The Egyptian Knowledge Bank and Scientific Databases

The Egyptian Knowledge Bank EKB is a free gateway to the world's largest scientific databases — memorize "who gets you to what":

🏦

The Knowledge Bank EKB

It offers online workshops ✅ p.68 (every Monday and Thursday at 9 pm), and from its Sources menu the user reaches Clarivate ✅ p.83. Launched in 2014 by presidential initiative, with 4 portals: readers, researchers, students, and kids (ages 6–14).

🧭

The Famous Access Chain

Scopus sources are reached through the ELSEVIER database ✅ p.71–73 (Sources ← All Sources ← ELSEVIER ← Scopus Database) · and EndNote Online is among Clarivate's products ✅ p.84 (⚠️ the word "free" is not stated).

👤

Merging Author Accounts

The Scopus database lets you merge more than one account of the same author ✅ p.78–80: search for the author ← Request to merge authors ← confirm the profile ← review the articles — and the account is free via the Knowledge Bank with the same EKB email.

🔣

ProQuest and Boolean Operators

Boolean operations CAN be used within ProQuest ✅ p.96 — the phrase "cannot" is wrong (a trap!). ProQuest provides full texts of dissertations since 1997 and more than 2 million theses, with translation into 18 languages.

🕌

Arabic Sources

The Dar Al-Mandumah index (a Saudi company, 2004) contains subsections ✅ p.101–103: EduSearch, HumanIndex, AraBase, EcoLink, and university theses · and the Arabic Citation Index (ACI) accepts searches in Arabic and English ✅ p.92 and 94 — not Arabic only (a trap!).

🤝

The Ejada Platform Ejada

The product of cooperation between the Al-Alfi Foundation for Human and Social Development and the Ministry of Higher Education and Scientific Research and the Supreme Council of Universities ✅ p.108 — training courses to develop youth skills (ejada.edu.eg).

📷

Unsplash Photos

All photos posted on Unsplash are free to copy, modify, and distribute — even for commercial purposes ✅ p.108 (literally) — part of Open Educational Resources (OER).

03. Journal Metrics — IF, SJR, and SCImago

How do we know a scientific journal is "reputable"? By its impact metrics — the most confusing part of this section:

⭐

Impact Factor Impact Factor

It is based on the number of Citations ("articles coming from the journal" — literally in the material, p.86) within a year — not the number of searches (a trap!). It is "a powerful tool if you want to compare journals in the same subject category" ✅ p.86.

📊

SCImago and SJR

SCImago is a portal built on the Scopus (Elsevier) database ✅ p.104 — it displays a journal's annual metrics and Quartiles ✅ p.106. And SJR is the indicator of journals' scientific influence ✅ (the material's glossary, p.109) — derived from Google's PageRank algorithm, with data since 1996.

🌍

WOS — Web of Science

Its advanced search narrows the scope "to specific criteria" ✅ p.85, and clicking a journal's name displays its impact factor ✅ p.86–87 — the material's example: JIF = 3.034 (2021) for IET Renewable Power Generation.

🗂️

Ranking and Filtering

In SJR: Journal Rankings ← field and category ← filters for subject, country, type, and year ✅ p.105 — so classifying by year is available, and the phrase "difficult to classify them by year" is wrong.

05. What the Memo Doesn't Cover

The official material adds a whole world of researcher tools:

🎓

Where This Module Sits in the Certificate

Internet Search is program BIT 06 (elective) — a two-day course with four sessions. The number of web pages reached 1.13 billion in January 2023 according to Netcraft.

🕷️

How Does a Search Engine Work? (p.17)

Three stages: Crawling (the crawler moves through links — a page with no links to it may never be discovered) ← storing data in an index ← Ranking. The 7 best-known engines: Google, Bing, Yahoo, Yandex, Baidu, DuckDuckGo, ASK.

🧩

The Five Parts of a URL (p.21–22)

Scheme (the protocol) · Authority (domain + port) · Path · Parameters (key/value pairs joined with &) · Anchor after the # — and the four protocols: HTTP, HTTPS, FTP (file transfer over TCP/IP), and SMTP (email).

✨

More Search Tricks (p.25–31)

site: to search within a site · filetype: for a file type · the asterisk * as a placeholder (great for song lyrics!) · two dots .. for a number range · quotation marks drop results from 1.05 billion to 133 thousand in the material's example · and Boolean operators are named in honor of the mathematician George Boole.

⚖️

Evaluating Source Credibility (p.38)

11 criteria, chiefly the author's credentials and content freshness · domain meanings: .gov government, .edu education, .mil military, .org organization · and social media sites are not suitable as references for researchers.

🎓

Google Scholar (p.41–48)

Requires a Gmail account · ranks documents by full-text weight, publication venue, author, and citation count · "Search in libraries" leads to Worldcat.org · citation formats: MLA, APA, Chicago, Harvard, and Vancouver.

🔬

Research Gate & EULC

RG is the largest academic social network (more than 20 million members — per Nature), and its RG Score measures scientific reputation · the Egyptian Universities Libraries Consortium (EULC) provides more than 70,000 journals and e-books.

🧮

Calculating JIF — the Material's Example (p.89–90)

The 2021 impact factor = 2021 citations of the previous two years' publications (2,242) ÷ citable items (739) = 3.034 · and the JCR report classifies journals into quartiles Q1–Q4 per subject category.

🕌

The Arabic Citation Index — ACI (p.91–94)

Funded by the Egyptian government via the Knowledge Bank · "the first regional citation index in Arabic", in partnership with Web of Science · linked to more than 1.7 billion references, some going back to 1864 · coverage from 2015 to the present.

📚

Open Educational Resources — OER (p.107–108)

UNESCO's definition: openly licensed materials that allow reuse at no cost · platforms: MIT OCW (full MIT curricula), BCcampus, Pressbooks (5,101 books), the Cleveland Museum of Art (30,000+ artworks), and Unsplash — plus Egypt's own Ejada platform.
