Memo Explanations

A simplified explanation of everything in the Networks section of the memo (28 questions).

01. Addresses and Protocols — Every Device's Identity

Every device on the network has two addresses: IP, its changeable logical address, and MAC, its fixed physical address engraved in the network card.

🏠 Analogy: An IP address is like your home address (it changes when you move to a new house), while a MAC address is like your national ID number (fixed wherever you go). The TCP mail carrier knocks and confirms the parcel arrived intact, while UDP tosses the parcel over the fence and walks away — faster, but with no guarantee.
🔢

IP Address

It consists of 4 fields (Octets), each from 0 to 255 ✅ p.44 — that is, 256 values per octet. The device's address must be in the same Subnet ✅ p.45–46: the network number is like the street name, and the host number like the house number.

🪪

MAC Address

The address used on the local Ethernet network ✅ p.44 — it consists of 12 characters with a dot between each pair, while the Internet uses the IP address.

🔄

DHCP — A Changing Address

Assignment is either manual (static) or automatic via a DHCP server ✅ p.44 — its four messages: Discovery ← Offer ← Request ← Acknowledge (p.67).

✅

TCP or UDP?

TCP stands out for accuracy (delivery confirmation, flow control, and error detection) while UDP stands out for speed without confirmation ✅ p.64–65 — attributing accuracy to UDP is a recurring trap!

🧅

The Four TCP/IP Model Layers

Application (HTTP/FTP/SMTP) ← Transport (TCP/UDP) ← Internet (IP and Routers) ← Link/Network Access (Ethernet up to 1 Gbps, and switches) ✅ p.64–65 — and the access layer defines the physical communication methods with the local network ✅.

🛠️

Tools & Systems

Wireshark monitors and analyzes data traffic in real time ✅ p.42 · Linux is free, open-source, and widely used for servers and networks ✅ p.40 · NFS was developed by Sun Microsystems in 1984 ✅ p.53 — Microsoft's counterpart is SMB.

02. Connecting Devices and the Star Topology

The Star topology is the most famous network structure: every device connects to one central point:

⭐

The Heart of the Star

The memo's answer: the Switch. ⚠️ The material's text (p.19): the central point is "usually a device such as a hub, switch, or router" — so stay flexible depending on the question's options. And the star topology is the most used of the three structures.

📉

Hub or Switch?

The Hub retransmits on all ports, causing collisions, while the Switch forwards only to the intended port, is faster, and sends and receives simultaneously ✅ p.36 — so the hub is less used (the phrase "more commonly" is a trap!).

🎴

Network Card NIC

The Network Interface Card is what translates digital data into electrical signals — attributing this to "connecting devices" in general is wrong (a trap!).

🖧

The Two Operating Models

Client-Server networks are the ones that require a server device with special specifications to provide the service — while in Peer-to-Peer networks the devices are equal, with no central server (a classic mix-up trap!).

03. Cables and Ports

This part's questions are small details to memorize as they are:

📺

HDMI Port

Used as a port to connect high-definition TVs.

🖥️

VGA Port

An old port — its image quality is lower, so the phrase "used in high-resolution displays" is wrong (a trap!).

🧵

Coaxial Cable

It consists of 4 parts ✅ p.32: a copper core, insulation, a braided metallic outer conductor, and a plastic jacket — not five (a trap!). It was used in the old days at 10 Mbps.

🔀

Crossover Cable

The crossover connects two similar devices (computer to computer) ✅ p.32 — while the Straight-through cable is for two different devices (computer to switch).

📡

Wireless Communications

Wireless Communications transmit data over electromagnetic waves.

04. Internet Connection Types and the Cloud

Who are leased lines for? Is DSL speed constant? And which cloud is the Windows platform?

🏢

Leased Lines

Leased Lines are for large companies ✅ p.49: higher security, high speed, and high cost, with dedicated protocols PPP and HDLC — not for individuals (a trap!).

📞

DSL Line

The memo's answer: the speed remains constant for the customer ✓ ("by the customer" is a preposition trap). ⚠️ The material (p.48) explains: speeds up to 100 Mbps decrease with distance from the exchange — its types are ADSL (most common at home), SDSL (symmetric), and VDSL (very high speed).

☁️

The Windows Cloud

Windows Azure is among the examples of Public clouds ✅ p.24 — alongside Google AppEngine and Amazon EC2. Public clouds are economical but less secure than private ones.

🛒

Amazon AWS

Among the examples of infrastructure (IaaS) providers ✅ p.25 (with Cisco Metapod and Google Compute Engine) — describing it as a "Software provider" is wrong (a trap!).

06. What the Memo Doesn't Cover

The official material is far broader than the memo's 28 questions — here is its essence:

🎓

Module Position & Geographic Classification

Introduction to Networks = BIT 07 (elective). Geographically, networks come in 5 types (p.14–16): PAN personal · LAN local · CAN campus (a group of LANs) · MAN a whole city (like the New Administrative Capital) · WAN wide-area, via telecom companies and satellites.

🚌

The Three Topologies (p.18–19)

Bus: collisions on simultaneous transmission, and cutting the cable stops the whole network · Ring: one direction, with every device acting as a signal repeater · Star: the most used — disconnecting one device doesn't affect the rest, and faults are easy to detect.

📶

Wi-Fi by the Numbers (p.20–21, p.60–61)

Short for Wireless Fidelity, its standards are IEEE 802.11, on the 2.4 and 5 GHz bands · 802.11ac up to 1 Gbps · Wi-Fi 4=n, 5=ac, 6=ax · 5 GHz is faster with less interference, while 2.4 GHz handles obstacles better.

📡

Cellular, NFC & RFID (p.22–23)

5G is 10 times faster than 4G (10 Gbps theoretically) · NFC: range not exceeding 4 cm at 474 Kbps — which is why it's financially safe · RFID: a Tag (passive or active) + a Reader — e-passports and goods tracking.

🧵

Cable Categories (p.31–32)

Twisted-pair has 8 wires with an RJ45 connector, in shielded STP and unshielded UTP types · Categories: Cat5 (100 Mbps) ← Cat5e (1000) ← Cat6A (10 Gbps) ← Cat8 (25–40 Gbps) · FTTH: OLT ← splitter (64 or 128 clients) ← ONU.

🔢

Address Classes (p.45–46)

Class A mask 255.0.0.0 · B 255.255.0.0 · C 255.255.255.0 · A host number of all zeros = the network address, and all ones = the Broadcast address · the default gateway is found with the ipconfig command.

🌐

The Internet's Birth & Connection Methods (p.47–50)

It began in 1969 as ARPANET with 4 computers supporting U.S. military research · Dial-up doesn't exceed 56 Kbps and occupies the phone line · Mobile generations: 1G voice only ← 2G digital + SMS ← 3G Internet ← 4G high speeds ← 5G + the Internet of Things.

📮

The Final Session's Protocols (p.66–68)

DNS: translates easy-to-remember domain names into IP addresses · DHCP: Discovery ← Offer ← Request ← Acknowledge · IMAP: accessing email messages and storing them for offline work — used by Outlook and Thunderbird · SMTP: sending mail.

🛡️

Network Security Tools (p.69–71)

Firewall monitors traffic and allows/blocks by rules · IPS blocks attacks by comparing them to previous ones in real time · NAC identifies every user and device · DLP prevents data from leaking outside the organization · VPN is a private encrypted virtual channel · and intrusion is the easiest attack to carry out.

🔐

Securing the Home Router (p.74–78)

Log in via 192.168.0.1 or 192.168.1.1 · the recommended encryption is WPA2-PSK · hide the network by disabling SSID Broadcast · and the WPS feature must be disabled to protect the router from intrusion.
