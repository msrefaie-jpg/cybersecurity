02. App Types and No-Code Building Platforms

Four types that keep appearing in questions, plus three no-code building platforms:

📲

Native Native Apps

Apps programmed for a particular operating system (iOS or Android only) — highest performance, most expensive to develop.

🌐

Cross-Platform Cross-Platform

Use one global language such as JavaScript and run on every system.

🚗

Hybrid Hybrid

A mix of native and web, displayed inside a WebView — the official material's examples: Evernote and UBER ✅ p.28. Drawback: very limited access to phone hardware.

🔗

Progressive Web Apps PWA

Their advantage is providing a website shortcut within the phone — they run from the browser like an app (the very app in your hands is a live example!).

🛠️ No-code building platforms (the material lists 10 — memorize what makes each stand out): Apps-builder: has published more than 500,000 apps ✅ p.25 (published apps — not "templates" as some phrasings suggest ⚠️) · Good Barber: control every detail "without a single line of code" ✅ p.25 · MobinCube: a powerful editor for relatively complex apps ✅ p.26 · Also from the curriculum: AppMakr (the 20-minute build challenge), App Machine (the easiest), and Shoutem (founded 2011, from $49/month).

03. Google Apps — Workspace (formerly G Suite)

The app family that appears most often in questions. The material (p.48): Workspace was "formerly known as Google Apps and later G Suite" — memorize the memo's answer for the exam (G Suite) and understand the current name:

🈯

Translate Translate

Instant translation mode is activated by tapping the Conversation icon and then speaking into the microphone.

👁️

Google Lens Lens

Photograph any product to learn its price and where it is sold — search with the camera instead of typing.

🗺️

Maps Maps

You can change the day or hour of travel to find the best route before leaving — and updating the app is recommended for new features.

📝

Keep Keep

Google's note-taking app — a direct answer to a memo question.

🎥

Meet Meet

Free meetings with up to 100 participants for 60 minutes ✅ p.70 — paid plans reach 500 participants and streaming to 100,000 viewers.

📅

Calendar Calendar

For creating events and scheduling appointments effectively ✅ p.49 — with a maximum of 5 reminders per event (email or notification), and it syncs with Outlook and Apple Calendar.

📍

Find My Phone Find My Phone

Activated automatically as soon as a Google account is added to your phone ✅ p.44 — conditions for locating: signed in + location on + data or Wi-Fi connection + visible on Google Play.

🛡️

Protection Google Play Protect

Google's security tool for scanning apps before and after installation — it stops and removes harmful apps and sends privacy alerts ✅ p.18.

04. Social and Media-Editing Apps

Numbers and facts that keep coming up in the exam:

✈️

Telegram

One-to-one messages or groups of up to 200,000 members ✅ p.94 — file size limit 2 GB, and secret chats use the MTProto protocol. ⚠️ Don't confuse this with "200 million monthly active users" (March 2018).

🎤

TikTok & Snapchat

The TikTok feature of filming next to another video is called Duet (the memo wrongly says "Deep Duo"). Snapchat: recording, broadcasting, and sharing video messages for a specific period of time.

👥

Facebook

Its three founders per the material: Mark Zuckerberg, Chris Hughes, and Dustin Moskovitz ✅ p.82 (no mention of Saverin). It expanded to include anyone aged 13 years or older ✅ p.82.

▶️

YouTube & WhatsApp

YouTube used to rely on Adobe Flash and now uses HTML5 ✅ p.88. WhatsApp is a centralized messaging service ✅ p.76 (the phrase "decentralized" is wrong) — with end-to-end encryption via the Signal protocol since April 2016.

🎨

Photo & Video Editing Family

Canva, Filmora, Lightroom, and Snapseed — all belong to photo and video editing apps (the memo keeps trying to trick you by attributing them to meeting or social apps!).

📧

BlueMail & Microsoft 365

BlueMail is a secure email app, free on both stores. Signing in with your academic university email unlocks the paid Microsoft 365 features for free.

05. Connectivity and Security on Your Phone

📶

Wi-Fi, Mobile Data & GPS

Wi-Fi connects your phone to the local network and the Internet (communication between two local devices = Wi-Fi, not mobile data). GPS does not rely on Internet data — it works via satellites. And the phone does not connect to the router via Bluetooth!

🔐

Payment Security

OTP: a passcode valid for a few minutes to secure banking transactions. The CVV on the back of the card must be seen by no one but the owner. Two-factor authentication (2FA): two keys to log in. Secure websites start with HTTPS. And the e-wallet: linking cards after passcode verification.

🔋

Battery & Memory

To lock an Android phone or erase its data: battery charge must be at least 50%. RAM controls how many apps run concurrently. Apps auto-starting at system boot affect system memory. Pre-installed system apps can be disabled, not deleted.

💳

MicroSD & QR

MicroSD is external storage memory — not a way to protect the screen (a famous trap). And a QR Code is a developed version, or second version, of the Barcode.

07. What the Memo Doesn't Cover

The official material is full of numbers and dates that haven't reached the memo yet:

🎓

Where This Module Sits in the Certificate

Mobile Applications is program BIT 08 (elective) — the certificate requires 5 mandatory + 2 elective programs out of 10, each program lasting two days.

📜

Smartphone Timeline (p.6)

1992 first smartphone, IBM Simon · 1996 Nokia 9000 · 2000 Sharp J-SH04, first built-in camera · 2003 BlackBerry Quark · 2007 Apple's first touch phone · 2008 HTC Dream, first Android · 2010 Galaxy S series.

🧠

Phone Components — Numbers to Memorize (p.7–9)

RAM: preferably 8 GB, no less than 4 · Storage: at least 128 GB (up to 512 on flagships) · Screen up to 6.9 inches (OLED/LED/AMOLED) · Battery in mAh · Camera: OIS optical stabilization, HDR, auto focus · Spec reference: gsmarena.com.

📡

Connectivity Technologies (p.10)

NFC: data transfer by touch within a range not exceeding 5 centimeters — the basis of contactless payment · Bluetooth: first pairing with a code, then automatic · Hotspot: sharing your phone's Internet with other devices.

🔐

Sensors & Security (p.9, p.17–19)

The Proximity sensor turns the screen off near the ear · ambient light sensor · fingerprint · Security tips: turn Bluetooth off completely rather than just making it "invisible", do a factory reset before selling the phone, and back up via Google Drive.

📍

"Find My Device" in Detail (p.44–45)

To lock the device or erase its data: battery charge at least 50% · "Play sound" makes it ring for 5 minutes at full volume even if silenced · erasing data is final but may not delete the SD card, and the service stops afterwards.

💬

Social Media Numbers

Twitter: 280 characters, founded 2006, Musk's $44 billion acquisition (2022) · Instagram: 10 photos max per post, 60-second video with longer via IGTV, Stories last 24 hours · Snapchat: Stanford students, a screenshot notifies the sender, and the username cannot be changed · TikTok: "Douyin" from ByteDance, live broadcasting requires 1,000 followers and age 16+, gifts 18+.

💳

Digital Wallets (p.112–115)

They keep data in encrypted form using the SSL protocol · Egypt's best wallets 2023: Phone Cash (NBE), Cairo Cash, Smart Wallet (CIB), E-Wallet (QNB), Vodafone Cash · 4 creation steps ending with linking the cards.

☁️

Google Drive & Co-Editing (p.57–63)

15 GB free, upgrades via Google One (100 GB up to 30 TB) · Egyptian academic email: unlimited storage · A file cannot be edited on more than 100 devices/tabs at once · Google Maps: up to 9 stops per trip.

🎦

Meet's Competitors (p.70–71)

Zoom: free up to 100 participants but only 40 minutes for more than two people · Free Conference Call: up to 1,000 people · Webex by Cisco · Teams integrates with OneDrive and SharePoint.
